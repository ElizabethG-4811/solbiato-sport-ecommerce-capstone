## Naming Patterns
- "K-" prefix is used for kids products
- "CL-" prefix appears on classic collection items
- Core (Sport) items often have no prefix
- Naming conventions reflect internal product segmentation and brand evolution

## Cultural & Naming Strategy
- Product names reflect local DC culture, slang, and lifestyle
- Some items appear to be named after people, places, or relatable activities
- Naming creates a sense of community and familiarity
- Kids and adult collections appear to share similar naming themes and visuals
- This suggests a unified brand identity accross age groups

## Design & Naming Relationship
- Product names are concept-driven and often reflect the visual design of the item
- Examples include names like "K-Smiley" etc.
- Designs appear to mimic or be inspired by the concept in the product name or current events (popularity)
- This creates a strong connection between branding and visual identity
- The brand blends local references with universal concepts and personal names

## Historical Context (2006-2010)
- The brand was primarily brick-and-mortar during this time
- Coordinated outfits (skull caps, shirts, pants, jackets) were commonly worn together
- Product matching appears to have been a consistent part of the brands identity
- It is unclear whether product naming was as descriptive or concept-driven as it is today
- Access to the brand was influenced by location (ex. Georgetown DC) and affordability
- The brand carried a sense of exclusivity within the community

## Product Ecosystem Insight
- Products are designed as part of coordinated systems rather than the standalone items
- Items across categories (tops, bottoms, accessories) share identical or highly similar visual designs
- Naming conventions often reflect relatiosnhips between items (ex. Triangle, Digits, Text, etc.)
- Accessories such as skull caps frequently match the same design patterns as clothing items
- This creates a full outfit ecosystem that encourages mutli-item purchases and cohesive styling

## Visual Merchandising Evidence
- Promotional materials display coordinated outfits with matching tops and bottoms
- Products are presented in complete looks rather than as standalone items
- Pricing is structured to encourage purchasing both tops and bottoms together ( sales of $69 tops, $59 bottoms as promotional deals)
- This reinforces the brands's "complete the look" strategy in its online marketing
